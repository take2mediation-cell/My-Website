# Best-Seller Criteria

A scoring rubric for judging whether a Claude Plugin idea has top-seller
potential.

> **Internal use only.** This rubric is how *you* decide which 5 ideas are the
> strongest and most worth presenting — and how you rank them. Do **not** show
> scores, factors, or sub-scores to the user. The user only ever sees the 5
> ideas in the clean Claude Plugin Idea Template format. Use this silently to
> pick and order them.

Use it two ways:

1. To **filter** the candidate ideas down to the 5 strongest, most *different*
   options.
2. To **rank** those 5 so the best-positioned idea leads.

Score each of the 7 factors 0–10, multiply by its weight, and sum to a
**Best-Seller Score (0–100)** — kept internal.

| # | Factor | Weight | What a 10 looks like |
|---|--------|:------:|----------------------|
| 1 | **Pain intensity** | 20 | The buyer feels this weekly and it costs them money, time, or status. They're already paying to solve it. |
| 2 | **Market size × reachability** | 18 | Large enough audience *and* the user can reach them cheaply through owned or paid channels. |
| 3 | **AI advantage** | 16 | The core task is recurring, judgment-light, and AI does it dramatically faster/cheaper/more consistently than the status quo. |
| 4 | **Speed-to-value** | 12 | Buyer gets a usable, "wow" output within minutes of first use — not after a setup project. |
| 5 | **Differentiation** | 12 | A specific, ownable angle (niche-fit, packaged workflow, proprietary framework) — not "a better X." |
| 6 | **Willingness to pay** | 12 | The outcome is worth real money and the price clears the value of what they pay now. |
| 7 | **Build feasibility** | 10 | Shippable as a Claude Plugin/Skill with current models and no heavy external infra. |

**Best-Seller Score = Σ (factor score ÷ 10 × weight).**

---

## Interpreting the score

- **80–100 — Strong best-seller candidate.** Pursue. Move to Offer DNA.
- **60–79 — Promising, needs sharpening.** Usually a differentiation, pricing,
  or reachability problem. Fix the weak factor before committing.
- **40–59 — Marginal.** Re-angle the idea or pick a different sub-niche.
- **< 40 — Weak.** The pain, reach, or AI advantage isn't there. Don't ship it.

---

## The best-seller pattern (what winners share)

The highest-scoring AI products almost always combine:

- **A sharp buyer** who already spends money on the problem.
- **A recurring, manual, "I dread doing this" task** as the target.
- **A fast, concrete, on-brand output** the buyer can use immediately.
- **A packaged workflow**, not a raw capability — the product makes the
  decisions a novice can't.
- **A price the buyer can say yes to without a meeting** (impulse-to-mid range
  for self-serve; reserve higher prices for clear ROI or done-for-you scope).

## Common best-seller killers

- **"Vitamin, not painkiller."** Nice-to-have. Low pain intensity → it won't
  sell, however clever.
- **Unreachable audience.** Great product, no affordable way to put it in front
  of buyers.
- **No AI advantage.** A template or checklist would do the same job — the AI
  is decoration.
- **Slow time-to-value.** Requires setup, data, or expertise before the buyer
  sees magic; they churn first.
- **Me-too.** Indistinguishable from three things they could already buy.
- **Priced against the wrong anchor.** Charging template prices for agency-level
  outcomes, or vice versa.

When an idea scores low, name the *specific* killer — it tells you exactly what
to change.
