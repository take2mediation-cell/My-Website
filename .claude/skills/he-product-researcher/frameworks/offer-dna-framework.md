# Offer DNA Framework

The **Offer DNA** is the single source of truth for a Claude Plugin sold as a
low-ticket AI product. It is read by the **Product Creator** (who builds the
plugin), the **Sales Funnel Specialist** (who writes the pages and emails), and
the **Meta Ads Specialist** (who writes ads) — so the whole launch is coherent.

**The Offer DNA must read like a one-page executive summary:** nicely
structured, organized, and formatted — easy for a human to read, understand, and
digest at a glance. Short lines. Clear labels. Scannable lists and tables. No
walls of text.

Render the final Offer DNA with `templates/offer-dna-template.md`, which has
exactly the **13 attributes** below in order. Fill every attribute. If something
can't be verified from research or user input, mark it `‹ASSUMPTION›` or
`‹NEEDS INPUT›` — never leave it blank, never fabricate proof.

> **Running example** (used throughout to show, not just tell): a Claude Plugin
> that writes a personalized, recruiter-ready resume. Product name: **Resume
> Rocket**.

---

## Formatting rules (apply to the whole document)

- **Exec-summary altitude.** Each attribute is a few tight lines, not paragraphs.
- **Label + description** for every list item: a bold 2–4 word label, an em dash,
  then one short line. Labels make it skimmable; descriptions make it land.
- **Buyer's words.** Pull verbatim language from the research into problems,
  outcomes, and the promise.
- **Outcome over feature.** Always pair what something *is* with what it *does
  for the buyer*.
- **Consistency.** 5–7 items wherever a range is given; keep parallel phrasing.

---

## The 13 attributes — how to write each

### 1. Product Name
**What:** The plugin's name. **Rules:** 3–5 words, catchy, attractive; it should
hint at the outcome or the "magic." Avoid generic/literal ("AI Resume Tool").
Prefer punchy, benefit- or metaphor-driven names. Offer 1–2 alternates.
**Example:** *Resume Rocket* (alt: *Interview Magnet*).

### 2. What It Is
**What:** A single sentence that says, plainly, what the plugin is and does.
**Use this exact formula:**

> **{Product Name}** is a **{category/role}** for Claude that **{capability 1}**,
> **{capability 2}**, and **{capability 3}** — so that you can **{benefit 1}**,
> **{benefit 2}**, and **{benefit 3}**.

The three capabilities are what the *plugin does*; the three benefits are what
the *buyer gets*. Keep verbs strong and concrete.
**Example:** *Resume Rocket is a personal resume writer for Claude that analyzes
the job you want, rewrites your experience to match it, and optimizes for ATS
filters — so that you can stop staring at a blank page, get past the robots, and
land more interviews.*

### 3. Big WOW Promise
**What:** The one promise the buyer can't ignore. **Formula:**

> **{Dream Outcome}** — without **{something the buyer hates}**.

The dream outcome is the headline result; the "without" removes the pain, cost,
or effort they dread. Make it bold and believable — the rest of the Offer DNA
must back it up.
**Example:** *A recruiter-ready resume in 60 seconds — without templates, writer's
block, or paying $300 to a resume service.*

### 4. Paradigm Shift
**What:** Reframes the problem so the buyer sees a new path. **Formula:**

> You **don't** need **{X — the old thing they assume they need}** to **{dream
> outcome}**. What you need is **{Y — the real lever}**.

X is the expensive/hard/slow thing they think is required; Y is the new,
plugin-enabled way. This makes the offer feel inevitable.
**Example:** *You don't need to hire a professional writer or learn "resume hacks"
to land interviews. What you need is a resume tailored to each specific job in
seconds.*

### 5. Old Way vs. New Way
**What:** A side-by-side contrast. **Old way = complex, expensive, slow,
ineffective. New way = fast, simple, easy, effective.** Keep the four pairs, and
add a specific detail to each side so it's concrete, not generic.
**Example:** Old: *Slow — hours per version.* New: *Fast — a tailored resume in
under a minute.*

### 6. Ideal Customer
**What:** Who this is for, in 1–2 tight sentences: who they are, what they want,
what they struggle with. Specific enough that the right person thinks "that's
me." Avoid "anyone who…".
**Example:** *Job-seekers applying to competitive roles who keep getting ghosted —
they know they're qualified but their resume isn't getting them past the filters.*

### 7. Problems Solved
**What:** The **5–7 biggest problems** the plugin removes. Each = **label + short
description**, in the buyer's own language. These map directly to FAQ and ad
angles later.
**Example:** *Blank-page paralysis — you don't know what to write or how to start.*
· *Generic resumes — one resume sent to every job, tailored to none.*

### 8. Dream Outcomes
**What:** The **5–7 biggest results / transformations** the buyer achieves. Each =
**label + short description** of the concrete, felt after-state. Mirror the
problems where natural (every pain has a payoff).
**Example:** *More interviews — your inbox starts filling with callbacks.* · *Total
confidence — you hit "apply" knowing your resume is the strongest in the stack.*

### 9. How It Works
**What:** Three dead-simple steps that prove how easy it is. Keep this exact
shape:
1. **Submit your info** — {what the buyer provides}
2. **Wait a few seconds** — {the plugin does the work}
3. **Receive your {creation}** — {the finished, personalized output}

Name the `{creation}` concretely (resume, plan, ad set, roadmap…).
**Example:** *1) Paste your old resume + the job link. 2) Wait a few seconds. 3)
Receive your tailored, recruiter-ready resume.*

### 10. Features
**What:** **5–7 capabilities of the plugin** (what it can *do*). Each = **label +
short description**. Distinct from #11: features are abilities, not the contents
of the output.
**Example:** *ATS Optimizer — rewrites phrasing to pass applicant-tracking filters.*
· *Job-Match Engine — tailors every bullet to the specific role.*

### 11. What Your Personalized {Creation} Includes
**What:** **5–7 things the buyer actually receives** inside their custom output —
the components of the deliverable, made just for them. This is the "what's in the
box" of the personalized creation (vs. #10, which is what the plugin can do).
**Example (the resume they get):** *A tailored headline + summary* · *Reworded,
quantified experience bullets* · *A matched skills section* · *A cover-letter
draft* · *An ATS-readiness score.*

### 12. Price Framing
**What:** The anchor and the offer. **Default: standard price $97, special price
$37** (adjust if the research or user input warrants). Show the standard price
struck through, the special price highlighted, and **one line on why it's a
no-brainer** (price vs. the value in #13, or vs. what they'd pay elsewhere).
**Example:** *~~$97~~ → $37 today. Less than a tenth of a $300 resume service —
for a better, faster result.*

### 13. Value Framing
**What:** Make the $37 feel trivial next to what they gain. Quantify across:
- **Time** — how much time they save or gain.
- **Money** — how much money they save or gain.
- **Feel / perform** — how much better they'll feel, perform, sleep, look, etc.
- **Bottom line** — the stacked value vs. the special price, in one line.

Use real numbers where you can; mark estimates as `‹ASSUMPTION›`.
**Example:** *Time: 6+ hours saved per application. Money: skip the $300 writer.
Feel: walk into every application with confidence. Bottom line: ~$300+ of value
and a weekend back — for $37.*

## Quality bar

- **Reads like an exec summary.** A busy human can skim it top-to-bottom in under
  a minute and "get" the whole offer.
- **Spec, not hype.** Every claim is something the plugin can actually deliver —
  the team that builds and backs it reads this.
- **Buyer's words throughout.** Problems, outcomes, and the promise echo the
  research verbatim.
- **Formulas followed.** Attributes 2, 3, 4, 5, 9 use their given shapes exactly.
- **Honest proof.** Never fabricate testimonials, results, or stats; flag gaps.
- **Hand-off ready.** When finished, the Offer DNA lets the Product Creator scope
  the build, the Funnel Specialist write the page, and the Ads Specialist write
  hooks — without re-interviewing the user.
