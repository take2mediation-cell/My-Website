# Email Body Templates (annotated)

Every example line in this file is a PATTERN. Do not reuse the wording. Derive the equivalent in the
user's voice from `references/voice-and-examples.md`.

Every high-performing sales email follows the same skeleton, with length and emphasis shifting by job.
The universal skeleton:

> **Personal hook -> reframe/teach -> proof -> offer -> one CTA -> deadline -> P.S. stack**

Teaching/story emails run the full skeleton long. Urgency/closer emails compress it to 4-6 lines. Below
are drop-in templates per email type. Keep the **offer block** identical across the whole sequence so it
reads consistent. No em dashes. One CTA, bolded.

Reusable **offer block** (define once, paste into each email):
```
[Offer block]
{Product name}: {one-line what-it-is}.
{N} {done-for-you assets / modules}. {Live component, e.g. 4 live Fridays with {host} + {partner}}.
Starts {date}, {time}. We {build / do} it together.
[ {CTA, e.g. ENROLL AT {discount} ->} ]
{Deadline mechanic, e.g. "Thursday EOD: price jumps ${X}."}
P.S. {3-pay option}.   P.P.S. Not for you? {suppression link}.
```

Every template below is delivered with this header immediately above the body. All lines are required,
every time:
```
Send: {day, time}  ·  Type: {type}
Subject A: ...   Subject B: ...   Preview: ...
Other preview candidates: {3 more subject lines beyond A and B, so 5 total}
```
Note: the clicker-safe behavior (dropping the discount number/current price on sends that touch
Clickers) still applies wherever a template below calls for it; it just isn't printed as a labeled
"Rule:" line anymore.

## A. Story / origin opener  (format #6, #5, the workhorse opener)
Job: create warmth + teach the reframe. Send first, to the engaged segment. Longest email of the set,
250-400 words (target ~325). The beats below are the minimum; each bracket is 1-3 full sentences, not a
single fragment, so a natural fill lands in range without padding.
```
{name},

{One concrete autobiographical scene, a time, place, and a number, written as 2-3 full sentences. e.g.
"5 years ago I was building my tenth attempt at this. The first nine failed. I'd sunk two years and most
of my savings into it."}

{The turn / realization, as 1-2 sentences.}

**{The reframe, bolded, the one worldview shift this whole launch teaches.}**
e.g. "Stop selling things people consume. Start selling things people install."

{One sentence unpacking what that reframe actually looks like in practice.}

{Proof: 2-3 hard numbers or a screenshot caption specific to this offer, written as 2-3 full sentences.}

{What it cost to keep doing it the old way, 1-2 sentences: time, money, or opportunity lost.}

That's the model {named authority + credibility, if you have one} and I packaged into {product}.

[Offer block]

{Personal sign-off line that ties back to the opening scene, 1-2 sentences.}
Best, {sender}
```

## B. Plain personal question opener  (format #1, highest opens)
Job: get opened on a numb list; offer a done-for-you outcome. Under 150 words.
```
{name},

Quick question. Can I {do the painful task} for you {before deadline}?

{1-2 lines: what the product does FOR them, framed as a service/outcome, not features.}

{Soft proof line.}

[ {CTA} ]
{light deadline}
Best, {sender}
```

## C. Product-launch / news  (format #2, highest unit volume)
Job: frame the offer as news + put the discount up front in preview. Medium.
```
{name},

{Product} {version} is here.

{2-3 lines on what's new / what it does, concrete.}
{Proof or screenshot.}

{The reframe in one line.}

[Offer block, lead the CTA with the discount, e.g. "GET IT AT {discount} ->"]
Best, {sender}
```

## D. Persona / product-writes-you  (format #3, highest single revenue)
Job: novelty + self-demonstration. Use once per launch. Written in the product's first-person voice.
250-400 words (target ~325).
```
hey {name}. it's {product/AI name}.

{The product narrates something it just did, ideally in real time, as 2-3 sentences. e.g. "I posted to
Instagram while you were reading this subject line. Then I wrote the caption for tomorrow's post too."}

{3-4 lines of what "I" can do for you, in the product's voice, each a concrete capability not a vague
claim.}

{A specific result or number this has produced, still in the product's voice, 1-2 sentences.}

{Hand back to the sender for the offer, or keep persona through the CTA, 1-2 sentences.}
[Offer block]
```

## E. Mechanism / "what it looks like"  (format #6, middle of sequence)
Job: remove vagueness, make the outcome believable. 250-400 words (target ~325). Often a clicker-touching
send (see rules: drop the discount number here).
```
{name},

A few of you asked what actually happens once you're in. Here's the plan, {step by step / week by week}.

*{Step 1, name + the asset they get + the result, 1-2 sentences.}*
*{Step 2, ...}*
*{Step 3, ...}*
*{Step 4, ...}*

{One sentence on what changes for them by the end of step 4 that wasn't true before they started.}

{Outcome line: what they HAVE at the end, 2-3 sentences. e.g. "By the end of the month you don't have a
folder of notes. You have a product that's live and selling while you sleep. You didn't have to figure
out the mechanics yourself."}

[ {CTA} ]
{deadline framed as "price goes up {day}" if a real discount ladder exists, or "doors close {day}, no
reopening until the next round" if the offer is a single flat price with no ladder, no discount number
on clicker sends either way}
P.S. {replay/guarantee reassurance}
```

## F. Social proof / "my team is obsessed"  (format #6, middle)
Job: proof + buyer-quality angle. 250-400 words (target ~325).
```
{name},

{Personal line: what you/your team are obsessed with or using right now, 1-2 sentences.}

{A specific result this has produced, 1-2 sentences with a real number if you have one.}

Here's why {the small product} beats {the obvious alternative}:
{The reframe as a contrast, 2-3 sentences. e.g. "A course asks people to watch, learn, apply. Most never
finish. A plugin asks them to install, run, get the result in minutes."}

{Buyer-quality angle: the kind of customer/result it brings, 1-2 sentences.}

{One more concrete detail: what a week or a day actually looks like using it.}

[Offer block]
Best, {sender}
P.S. {3-pay}. P.P.S. {suppression link}.
```

## G. Disqualifier  (format #7, middle, reframes + filters)
Job: reverse pressure, let the right reader self-select. Under 150 words, two bullet lists.
```
{name}.

Quick gut check before {the deadline}.

**This isn't for you if:**
- {painful "wrong fit" 1}
- {2}
- {3}

**This *is* for you if:**
- {right-fit 1}
- {2}
- {3}

If that's you, {product} is built for exactly that.
[ {CTA} ]
{deadline}
```

## H. Urgency  (format #4, late)
Job: re-tell the pattern in 3 lines, then push the deadline + introduce VIP/guarantee. 80-180 words
(target ~130).
```
{name},

{The pattern/proof compressed to 3 sentences, specific to this offer.}

{Tonight/the deadline}: {price moves / +$X / discount disappears}.

{One value line: "for ${price} you get {the offer}. Build one {asset} that does ${X}/day, it pays for
itself in {N} days."}

{One more sentence on what happens if they wait: the old way keeps costing them, or the price/discount
they lose.}

{Optional, first VIP mention: the upgrade + a strict one-line guarantee mention, under 15 words, e.g.
"14-day guarantee, no questions asked." Never the full risk-reversal paragraph here, that's reserved
for the final 1-2 emails only, see Escalation rules in sequence-and-rules.md.}
[ {CTA} ]
```

## I. Final call (tight clock)  (format #4, last day AM)
Job: pure harvest. 80-180 words (target ~130). Often a clicker-touching send (see rules: drop the
discount number and current price here, same as Template E). "Pure harvest" describes the job, not the
length, a bare 3-line email undershoots the floor; use the beats below.
```
{name},

Short one. {We go live / cart closes} {today/tomorrow}, {time}.

Quick recap: {the offer, compressed to 1-2 sentences: what they get + the core outcome}.

{One sentence on what it costs to keep putting this off, tied to the reframe used earlier in the
sequence.}

If you've been meaning to join, today is the day.

[ {CTA} ]
{deadline framed as "price goes up {day}" if a real discount ladder exists, or "doors close {day}, no
reopening until the next round" if the offer is a single flat price with no ladder, no discount number
and no current price on clicker sends either way}

P.S. {plugins/material waiting inside the moment you join, 1-2 sentences}.
```

## J. PM reopen / "this just happened"  (format #4/#9, final send, clickers only)
Job: event recap + "you haven't missed it." Send evening of close, to clickers only. 80-180 words, or
max 100 words if this is sent the same day as an AM final-call (Template I). Clicker-touching send: no
discount number and no current price, same as Templates E and I.
```
{name},

Psssss... so that just went live.

We ran {product} live for the first time today, and {social proof, packed room, who showed up}.
{2-3 screenshot placeholders: room/attendee count, chat, a result built live.}

This is the last time I'm going to invite you to this one.

And here's the part that matters: you haven't missed it. Join now and you can {catch the replay + do the
rest live}.
[ {CTA, JOIN BEFORE MIDNIGHT ->} ]
{deadline framed as "price goes up at midnight" if a real discount ladder exists, or "doors close at
midnight, no reopening until the next round" if the offer is a single flat price with no ladder, no
discount number and no current price on clicker sends either way}
P.S. {guarantee}, the risk is on us, not you.
```

## Length + CTA discipline
- Story / mechanism / social-proof / persona: 250-400 words, one CTA. **Target ~325 words** when drafting,
  not the low end of the range.
- Urgency / final-call / PM reopen: 80-180 words, one CTA. **Target ~130 words** when drafting, not the
  low end of the range.
- A PM final call (Template J) sent the same day as an AM final call (Template I): max 100 words (no
  floor override for this specific case).
- Question opener (Template B) and disqualifier (Template G): under 150 words.
- **Mandatory before delivering any email: count its actual words and compare to the range above.** If
  it's under the floor, that email is not done, go back and add another concrete line (more proof, more
  of the stakes, a second specific detail, a second beat of the story) until it's inside the range. Do
  not treat "short," "compressed," or "pure harvest" as permission to undershoot the floor; those words
  describe the job, the number is the actual requirement. A first draft that comes in under the floor is
  normal and expected to happen; delivering it without revising is the error.
- Never put two competing offers or two CTAs in one email.
- Bold the reframe line and the CTA. Keep paragraphs to 1-3 lines.
