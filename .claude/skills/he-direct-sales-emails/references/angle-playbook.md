# Angle Playbook (ranked email formats)

These are the email formats that reliably move money, ranked with the reasoning behind each so you
choose angles based on what works, not vibes. List sizes and audiences vary, so treat the ranking as a
guide to *why* a format works, not a guarantee of an exact number for any given list.

## How to use this file

Match the angle to the slot in the funnel:
- **Open** a promo with formats #1, #2, #3, or #5 (personal / question / launch-news / story). These get
  opened and they create the warmth the rest of the sequence harvests.
- **Middle**: mechanism/proof (#6) and the disqualifier (#7) to reframe and filter.
- **Close**: hard-urgency (#4). Never open cold with a deadline.

Most launches should braid 3-4 of these, not repeat one.

---

## 1. Plain personal question  ★ highest opens, quietly high volume
A bare, lowercase, first-name subject with a direct question in the preview. Looks like a 1:1 email, not
a campaign. Use it to OPEN a promo.

- `Hey {name}` -> "Can I automate {their painful task} for you before {deadline}?" (gets the highest
  opens of any format in this playbook, plus a solid run of sales)
- `Hey {name}` -> "Can I {do the task} for you?" (one of the top formats by unit volume on its own)
- `can I help you {get the outcome}?` -> "follow these 3 steps first"

**Why it works:** zero hype, reads as personal, so it gets opened on a list that is numb to promos. The
question implies a service/outcome done *for* them.

## 2. Product-launch announcement + discount in preview  ★ highest unit volume
Frame the offer as NEWS ("X 2.0 is here"), put the deep discount in the preview. Outsells "buy my thing"
because it reads as an update, not a pitch.

- `Your {product} 2.0 is here` -> "hire your new {category} now (at a deep discount)" (the top unit-count
  performer in this playbook, and it repeated strongly on a re-run)
- `like {familiar tool}, but {differentiator}` -> "{product}: new update!"

**Why it works:** newness + a concrete "it exists now" + a deep, specific discount. Use whenever there is
anything new, updated, or re-released.

## 3. AI-persona / "the product writes you"  ★ highest single-email revenue
The product (or its AI) speaks in first person and demonstrates itself inside the email.

- `hey {name}. it's {product/AI name}.` -> "I {did a concrete thing} while you were reading this subject
  line" (the top revenue performer in this format)

**Why it works:** novelty you cannot ignore, and it proves the product by doing the thing. Use once per
launch as your highest-ceiling creative swing.

## 4. Hard urgency / tight-clock closer  ★ the harvester
Short email, tight countdown, deadline mechanic. This is where back-half revenue lives. The tighter the
clock, the better.

- `[{N} hours!] then GONE` -> "last chance tbh"
- `Final countdown: {offer} ends tonight` -> "GONE at midnight"
- `final warning {name}` -> "act in the next 60 minutes"
- `final chance {name}` -> "{N} mins till the code expires"
- `this closes tonight` -> "last chance to join"

**Why it works:** it does not persuade, it forces a decision among people already convinced. Always stack
2 of these on the final day (a morning "X hours left" and an evening "60 minutes" / "we're live").

## 5. Vulnerable / playful personal  ★ your voice advantage
Casual, lowercase, human. Almost nobody else emails like this, so it stands out and opens high.

- `this scares me {name}` -> "should I press it or not?"
- `u up {name}?` -> "Me at 11:59PM refreshing the sales page..."
- `see you next year or...?` -> "{event} doors closing today"

**Why it works:** it is a pattern-interrupt in a promo-heavy inbox and it is unmistakably a real person.
Great opener or soft-nudge mid-sequence.

## 6. Outcome / provocation + mechanism
Bold result as the hook, with the offer/price in the preview; or a "here's exactly how it works"
breakdown that removes vagueness.

- `Cancel your marketing agency tomorrow` -> "{N}+ done-for-you assets. {price} before {deadline}"
- `2x-3x your business in 6-12 months` (outcome-led A/B variant)
- Mechanism body example: "what each Friday looks like" -> Research -> Creation -> Funnel -> Ads, ending
  in "a product that's live and selling while you sleep."

**Why it works:** the provocation earns attention; the mechanism makes the outcome believable. Use
provocation to open, mechanism in the middle once interest exists.

## 7. Disqualifier / contrarian  ★ reframes and filters
Tell them it might NOT be for them. Reverses sales pressure.

- `you probably don't need everything` (site-wide sale; consistently a strong unit-volume performer)
- `this isn't for you if...` -> bullet list of who it is and isn't for, then deadline + CTA

**Why it works:** removing pressure builds trust and makes the qualified reader self-select in. Put one in
the middle of every sequence.

## 8. Scarcity (spots / cohort) - for higher-ticket / cohort offers
- `opening {N} spots` -> "{aspirational lifestyle line}" (lifestyle proof in the preview, real cap on
  spots)

**Why it works:** a real, countable limit plus an aspirational lifestyle hook. Only use when the scarcity
is true.

## 9. Curiosity link (post-webinar / warm)
- `Here's the secret link we mentioned...` -> "Expires soon" (one of the strongest click-rate performers
  in this playbook when sent to a warm, post-event audience)

**Why it works:** open loop + "as promised" framing to a warm audience. Use right after a webinar/event.

## Format-to-slot cheat sheet

| Funnel slot | Best formats |
|---|---|
| Email 1 (open) | #1 personal question, #5 vulnerable, #6 story/provocation |
| Email 2 (why now) | #2 launch-news, #3 persona, #6 mechanism |
| Email 3 (proof/value) | #6 mechanism, social proof |
| Email 4 (reframe) | #7 disqualifier |
| Email 5-6 (urgency) | #4 hard urgency, #8 scarcity, guarantee/VIP |
| Final send(s) | #4 tight-clock closer (stack 2 on the last day), #9 if post-event |

## What did NOT belong (excluded from "direct sales")
Webinar invite/showup/registration, value emails, internal newsletter content, replays/reminders of
webinars, and customer-update/delivery emails. They can carry revenue but they are not direct-sales
emails, so do not model sales copy on them.
